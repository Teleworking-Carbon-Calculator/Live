Carbon impacts of homeworking

Homeworking (or working from home) is becoming an increasingly popular work style, promoted for presumed environmental benefits and a better work life balance. However due to changes in heating and electrical usage patterns alongside additional equipment requirements, these environmental benefits are far from assured. This website provides some best practice advice for anyone homeworking, and a simple calculator to estimate individual emissions under different scenarios.

Heating: Best practice

  • Heat a single room: Only heating occupied rooms significantly reduces energy consumption.
  • Keep temperatures below 20°C: Small temperature increases require far more energy.
  • Telework when the house is already occupied: If someone else is at home already using the heating, homeworking on those days will not meaningfully increase heating emissions.
  • If your heating emissions are too high, consider avoiding homeworking in winter months.

Electrical: Best practice

  • Avoid use of air conditioning: Desk fans have low energy usage however if air conditioning is required consider working from a shared office.
  • Make use of natural light: Lighting demand is small but often not offset by office savings.
  • Consider switching to a renewable energy supplier: Renewable electricity has a far lower impact than the standard grid mix.

Transport: Best practice

  • Use active travel on office days: Active transport has a very low carbon impact compared to driving.
  • Avoid relocating further away from the office after switching to hybrid work: Teleworkers who relocate often have higher transport emissions even when making fewer trips.
  • Carbon savings from homeworking are unlikely if you walk or wheel to work.
  • High frequency homeworking (3-5days/week): maximises carbon savings from transport.

Equipment: Best practice

  • Buy only what you need: a new 24" monitor has the equivalent carbon impact as 500 miles driven in a petrol car.
  • Prefer refurbished or second-hand: This eliminates the embedded carbon necessary in manufacturing new equipment.
  • Share equipment between all workstations: Using the same laptop and peripheries in the home office and regular work prevents duplication of embedded carbon.
